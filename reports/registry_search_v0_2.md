# Target-directed registry search (Sprint 2, Task B)

**Scope:** 20 targets whose atlases never ran one — 14 brake-atlas targets and the 6
alphacognition lead targets. The power atlas had already done this in Sprint 1 and is
excluded. **1,534 registered studies classified** across 67 search queries.

> ### ⚠ Corrected 2026-09-18 — the first version of this report understated everything
>
> The search fetched **one page of 50 records per query and never paginated**, then
> recorded the registry's `totalCount` as `total_hits`. Records therefore read as
> `total_hits: 1934 / kept: 25` — as though 1,934 studies had been screened. Twenty-five
> had been kept out of the **50** actually examined, and 1,884 were never fetched. A
> completed trial with posted cognitive results sitting at rank 51 was invisible and
> indistinguishable from one that did not exist.
>
> This is the project's recurring bug arriving from a new direction: **un-examined records
> silently converted into screened-and-rejected ones.**
>
> | | before | after |
> |---|---|---|
> | studies classified | 360 | **1,534** |
> | reporting gaps (completed, unreported) | 8 | **72** |
> | trials with posted results awaiting extraction | 5 | **40** |
>
> Two further classifier defects were fixed at the same time, both of which manufactured
> a "tested" state out of a record that showed no such thing:
>

## Why this mattered

Sprint 1 graded `human_intervention_evidence: 0` for these targets on the strength of
**literature** searches. The power atlas, forced by resolution R11 to declare what it had
actually searched, then ran registry searches and **overturned two of its own published
statements** — it had asserted that no human klotho intervention and no human MCT1
intervention existed, and both were wrong.

A registry holds trials that ran and were never published. That is precisely the category
a human-intervention facet depends on, and precisely the one a literature search cannot
return. So the repository's zeros were less trustworthy than its ones.

## Classification

The brief's seven states, as found:

| Code | State | Count | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | tested_and_positive | **0 assigned** | see below — not assigned mechanically |
| B | tested_and_null | **0 assigned** | see below |
| — | results_posted_extraction_pending | **40 trials** | posted results with a cognitive outcome; extracted verbatim, direction left to judgement |
| C | tested_wrong_endpoint | **1,090** | the trial RAN and measured no cognitive outcome at all |
| D | completed_unreported | **72 trials** | completed, cognitive outcome in protocol, nothing posted |
| E | terminated | **111** | `whyStopped` captured where the registry gives it |
| F | withdrawn | **45** | frequently zero enrolment |
| G | no_matching_trial_found | **3 targets** | CRTC1, OTX2, RTN4R — each on a **complete** screen |
| N | not_yet_run | **95** | status not in `RAN_STATUSES`; has measured nothing at all |
| U | outcomes_not_recorded | **15** | no outcome measures in the registry entry; protocol unknown |

**G, N and U are statements about records, not about biology.** G was previously named
`no_trial_identified` and its record text read "NOBODY HAS TESTED THIS" — a claim about
the world drawn from one registry, one set of terms, and a truncated slice of even that.
It is now `no_matching_trial_found`, and each record names the six registries that were
**not** searched (EudraCT, ISRCTN, jRCT, ChiCTR, ANZCTR, CTRI).

The three G targets are nonetheless *stronger* evidence of absence than before, because
their screens are now exhaustive rather than truncated: CRTC1 21 of 21 records examined,
OTX2 2 of 2, RTN4R 15 of 15. Within ClinicalTrials.gov, with these terms, there is
genuinely nothing. That still may not raise any facet's `counter_level`.

**A and B were deliberately not assigned by the pipeline.** Positive-versus-null is a
judgement about a prespecified endpoint, an effect direction and a comparison. A regex
over a results table cannot make it, and a pipeline that pretended to would be
manufacturing the exact kind of claim this repository exists to prevent. The **40** trials
with posted cognitive results are queued for verbatim extraction into
`evidence/registry_searches/results/` for the red team to classify with a reason. Five
were extracted before the pagination fix; the remaining 35 are outstanding.

## The three targets with no registered trial: G is not a null

`CRTC1`, `OTX2` and `RTN4R` returned **no registered trial** under any of their search
terms. Each record states, in the record itself:

> NO TRIAL MATCHED THESE TERMS in ClinicalTrials.gov within the records examined. This is
> a statement about one registry and one set of search terms, NOT a biological null and
> NOT a claim that the target is untested: other registries were not searched,
> unregistered work is invisible here, and a trial phrased differently would not match. It
> may not be recorded as evidence against the target, and it may not raise any facet's
> counter_level.

No `counter_level` anywhere was raised by this search. That is the whole discipline of
the exercise.

## Independent confirmation of the sprint's headline result

The GABRA5 basmisanil trial `NCT02953639` — which Sprint 1's brake atlas declared it
could not retrieve, and which the red team then retrieved manually — was picked up by
this pipeline independently and its results extracted:

* primary outcome, MATRICS Consensus Cognitive Battery at week 24: treatment differences
  `-0.36` (p = 0.730) and `+0.28` (p = 0.793);
* secondary MCCB domain scores: p = 0.556, 0.848, 0.973;
* Wechsler Memory Scale verbal: p = 0.211, 0.787.

That matches the red team's figure (+0.28, p = 0.793) reached by a different route. The
replicated human null on the repository's most-studied brake target now has two
independent retrieval paths behind it.

## Two classifier bugs found by reading the output

Both were found by reading extracted rows rather than trusting counts, and both had
inflated the findings by roughly half:

1. **`wais` matched inside "waist circumference".** Substring matching classified three
   metabolic secondary outcomes from KarXT trials as cognitive endpoints and queued them
   for cognitive results extraction. Short instrument acronyms — `wais`, `wisc`, `iq`,
   `moca`, `mmse` — are exactly the keywords most likely to appear inside an unrelated
   word. Fixed with word-boundary matching; reporting gaps fell from 22 to 11 and the
   extraction queue from 11 to 6.
2. **Matching a mechanism phrase by its first token.** "integrated stress response"
   matched a behavioural "Community **Reintegration** Workshop" — a trial with no
   pharmacological relation to EIF2S1. Requiring the exact phrase then over-corrected and
   dropped a genuinely relevant trial titled "…Dopamine **Receptor 1** Partial
   Agonist…", because registries do not phrase mechanisms the way we do. Settled on
   all-tokens-any-order with word boundaries.

## Recall limits, stated plainly

This search is **term-bounded**. Each record lists every term used, its provenance and
its per-term hit count, so the boundary is visible rather than implied. A target marked
`no_trial_identified` means *not found with these terms*, and the terms for targets with
no known modulator are necessarily weak — a gene symbol and a mechanism phrase. Three
specific limits:

* The registry indexes interventions, not genes. `RGS14`, `KCNQ3` and `HCN1` returned 1
  study each, and `GPR158` five, which reflects the absence of named clinical modulators
  rather than an exhaustive assessment.
* Only ClinicalTrials.gov was searched. EU CTR, ISRCTN, ChiCTR, JPRN and WHO ICTRP were
  not, so a trial registered only outside the United States is invisible here.
* `tested_wrong_endpoint` dominates at 1,090 of 1,534 studies. That is informative — most
  registered trials touching these mechanisms never measured cognition — but it is also
  where classifier precision matters most, and it rests on the keyword list in
  `pipelines/registry_search/target_search_terms.yaml`.
